# Schema Challenge Protocol — m1-max-64gb-purchase
## Loop Engineering addendum v4 — 2026-07-02

> Runs after gate analysis AND bias mitigation. This layer probes for
> maladaptive core beliefs and cognitive schemas that override rational verdicts.
>
> Triggered when: user expresses doubt despite clean gate results, or when
> emotional override patterns are suspected.
>
> Method: CBT-informed schema challenge. Not therapy — structured doubt interrogation.
> Each question maps to a specific schema distortion.
>
> Outcome:
>   - All RESOLVED → verdict holds (STRONG BUY confirmed)
>   - Any UNRESOLVED → loop reopens, schema must be named and challenged before proceeding

---

## Schema → Targeted Question Mapping

### 1. Emotional Override
*"A deeply held core belief triggers anxiety/fear, making you second-guess even when facts support the decision."*

**Q1:** What is the specific *feeling* that rises in your chest when you imagine completing this purchase? Name it in one word. Now ask: is this feeling protecting you from a real harm, or is it protecting you from a *belief* about harm (e.g., "I don't deserve this," "I'll mess it up")?

**Q2:** If this feeling were a person in your ear, what would it be saying verbatim? Write the exact sentence. Then ask: has this exact sentence stopped you from doing something beneficial before? How many times?

---

### 2. Cognitive Dissonance
*"When action conflicts with core belief, the mind creates discomfort and may dismiss evidence to resolve it."*

**Q3:** What core belief about yourself does spending ₹1.3L on a laptop *conflict* with? Examples: "I'm responsible with money," "I only spend on necessities," "I'm not the kind of person who invests in myself." Name yours.

**Q4:** You've done extensive analysis confirming this is sound. Yet doubt persists. What part of you *wants* this purchase to be wrong? What would you gain from proving it's a bad idea?

---

### 3. Overgeneralization
*"Maladaptive schemas amplify worst-case scenarios. Logic says 'sound decision' but schema says 'everything will go wrong.'"*

**Q5:** When you imagine this purchase going wrong, do you see *this specific machine failing* or do you see *your whole life cascading* — family disappointment, financial ruin, career collapse? If it's the cascade, that's overgeneralization. One bad event does not predict total life failure.

**Q6:** What's the *smallest* realistic negative outcome from this purchase? Not the worst case — the *minimum* harm. Is that minimum harm survivable? If yes, your threat assessment may be inflated.

---

### 4. Lack of Permeability
*"Rigid schemas resist new evidence. The brain clings to old patterns because they feel familiar, even when harmful."*

**Q7:** Is there a time in the past when you made a well-researched purchase that turned out well, but you still default to "I make bad decisions with money"? If yes, that's a rigid schema — one positive data point should update the pattern, but it hasn't. Why?

**Q8:** What would it take for you to look back at this purchase in 2 years and say "that was the best decision I made that year"? Not the full outcome — just the *standard of evidence* you'd need to see. Now ask: do you have any reason to believe that standard won't be met?

---

### 5. Schema-Driven Risk Inflation
*The specific pattern where past trauma or learned behavior makes you over-weight certain risks.*

**Q9:** Was there a past financial mistake — yours or someone close to you — where a large purchase led to real harm? If yes, that experience is being imported into *this* decision as if it were the same scenario. Is it the same scenario?

**Q10:** You have a backup PC. You have savings buffer. You have no EMI. The structural risks that likely caused past harm (debt, no buffer, no fallback) are *not present here*. Is your doubt responding to the actual current situation, or to a ghost of a past situation?

---

## Protocol

1. User answers all 10 schema-challenge questions.
2. Each answer evaluated: RESOLVED (schema named and separated from current reality) or UNRESOLVED (schema still driving the doubt).
3. Any UNRESOLVED → loop reopens with targeted reframing exercise.
4. All 10 RESOLVED → verdict holds. Buy with clarity.

**Important:** This protocol is not therapy. It's a decision-quality check. If persistent schema-driven doubt remains after this protocol, that's a signal to consult a professional — not to indefinitely delay a sound purchase.
